Did the 2025 four-day week pilots actually reduce burnout?

Mostly yes, though the effect is smaller and less even than the headlines suggest.

In the largest 2025 pilot, the share of staff reporting burnout fell from 41% to 29% over six months, measured with a standard 16-item inventory rather than a single survey question.

Sick days fell too, by about 22% on average, with the biggest drops in small teams. Most firms kept the schedule: 43 of 48 were still on it a year later.

The caveat is unpaid spillover. A third of managers say they answer email on the day off, so the gain is real for most staff but thinner for the people running the teams.
