# Gumdrop buttons

## Usage notes

- Use it in products with a friendly, consumer voice (habits, learning, reading, creator tools) where pressing a button should feel physical. Import the primitives (`GumdropButton`, `GumdropIconButton`, `GumdropSplitButton`, `StreakCard`); `GumdropButtons` is the assembled set for previews and docs.
- Buttons take a label and an optional leading icon. Async work is the promise returned from `onClick` (or a controlled `status`). The split button takes `{ id, label, hint?, icon? }` items. The streak card takes the days in a row before today, the personal best, seven day states and whether today is logged.
- Keyboard: every control is a native `<button>`, and Enter or Space squash it exactly like a pointer press. The chevron is a WAI-ARIA menu button: Enter, Space or ArrowDown open the menu on the first item and ArrowUp on the last; inside, the arrows wrap around, Home and End jump, a letter jumps by type-ahead, Escape closes and returns focus to the chevron, Tab closes and moves on, and choosing an item returns focus to the chevron. A click outside closes it.
- Screen readers: icon-only buttons have an `aria-label`, and the bookmark is a toggle with `aria-pressed`. Loading and success are announced once through a polite live region beside each button, and a busy button is `aria-disabled`, so a second press does nothing. The streak card reads "12 day streak", the personal best and each day's state ("Friday: today, not logged yet"); the number strips, flame and gumdrops are decorative and hidden.
- Focus is a 2px ring 3px outside each pill that also encloses the lip; on split segments the divider closes the ring. In light mode the ring is the accent shaded toward the text color, so it keeps 3:1 on the tinted tray.
- Responsive: the set uses container queries. From 1024px of container width the card overlaps the tray's top and bottom edges beside the buttons; from 704px to 1024px it sits inside the tray as a second column, with the button rows wrapping in pairs beside it; below 704px it stacks under the tray at nearly full width and overlaps its bottom edge evenly. Each row is two pairs that wrap together, so nothing is orphaned, and at 375px nothing scrolls horizontally. Small buttons grow to 40px on touch screens.
- States: hover lifts the button 2px on a taller lip and lightens the face; active sinks it into the lip and squashes it; focus shows the ring; disabled is flat, with a dashed outline and a muted label; loading keeps the exact width and shows three bouncing dots, which pause when scrolled out of view; success morphs the label into a check that draws itself, then returns to idle after 1.6s (the streak card rolls 12 to 13, then after 1.2s settles the button into a tinted "Logged today" pill that no longer lifts or looks primary, and stays aria-disabled). `forceState` renders hover, focus and active statically.
- Motion is one spring (stiffness 400, damping 18) written as a CSS `linear()` curve: a press lands in 90ms and the release settles in about 620ms with one visible overshoot. The icon wiggle, flame hop and marker pop are one-shot Web Animations that run only on interaction. With reduced motion everything is instant and the dots stand still.

## Taste self-review

- One clear focal point: Yes. The streak card's big "12" and its flame are the largest, boldest marks on the page; the button rows sit quietly on the tray as the supporting set, and the accent is spent only on primary actions, done days and the flame.
- Spacing on the 4px scale: Yes. Paddings, gaps and offsets are 4px multiples (8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 40, 48, 56 and 80px), button and card padding scale with the density token, and the only off-grid numbers are optical em trims on the big figure and the ring's 3px offset.
- Belongs in a Linear, Stripe or Vercel product: Yes. It is playful without being loose: one accent with one tint, a real state for every control, a menu button that follows the APG keyboard pattern, and motion that only answers a press or a change of state.
- Meets its category brief: Yes. Every interaction state renders statically (default, hover, focus, active, disabled, loading, success); loading buttons keep their width because the label never leaves the layout; and every icon-only button has an accessible name.
- Distinct from every other design in its category: Yes. It is the first design in Cards and buttons and the only one in the playful family: lip-and-squash candy buttons on a CSS spring, a split button with a full menu, and a habit-streak card with gumdrop day markers, laid out as a tinted tray with the card overlapping its edge.
